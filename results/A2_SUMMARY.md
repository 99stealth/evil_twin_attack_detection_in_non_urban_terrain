| **LOPO** | angle | MLP (Base) | 11.57 | 11.63 | [10.21, 12.93] | 10.52 |
| **LOAO** | xy | KNN (Base) | 3.78 | 0.00 | [2.81, 4.79] | 3.41 |
| **LOAO** | angle | Extra Trees (FE) | 3.67 | 1.41 | [2.86, 4.49] | 3.02 |

Reference lines: **random guess on the ring ≈ 14.0 m** (4R/π); **nearest-centroid
classification under LOPO ≥ 8.42 m** by construction (R2.2a); **classification under
LOAO ≈ 3.32 m** (F5, Task A1).

Sanity check (untuned-equivalent nested Extra Trees, FE, angle variant — the exact
scenario specified in the task): **LOPO mean 12.90 / median 12.71 m** (spec: ~12.9/~12.8)
and **LOAO mean 3.67 / median 1.41 m** (spec: ~3.7/~1.4) — matched closely.

**Central finding, reported as-is:** every model's **LOPO** error (10.9-13.8 m across
all 40 combinations) sits at or just below the 14.0 m random-guess line — **the
regressors do not interpolate to unseen positions on the ring.** The two ring-neighbours
at ±45° being in the training set does not help; models effectively guess. **LOAO**
error is far lower (3.7-5.2 m mean), comparable to the classification error (3.32 m,
F5) — the models generalize fine across antennas at a *seen* position, just not across
*unseen* positions. Figures: `figures/localization_error.png` (box plots, LOPO vs. LOAO,
all 5 models, angle variant) and `figures/lopo_predictions.png` (true vs. predicted
positions for the best LOPO model — predictions scatter around the full ring rather than
clustering near the true held-out point, visually confirming the negative finding).

## R2.2 — Localization baselines

Full table: `results/localization_baselines.csv`. FE features (KNN, nearest centroid),
Base features (WCL, trilateration — RSSI-only methods).

| Method | LOPO mean (m) | LOPO median (m) | LOAO mean (m) | LOAO median (m) | Position-dependent? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Nearest centroid | 13.03 | **8.42** | 3.72 | 0.00 | No |
| (b) WCL (raw) | 10.81 | 11.05 | 10.81 | 11.05 | Yes (provisional) |
| (b) WCL (projected to ring) | 12.89 | 15.32 | 12.89 | 15.32 | Yes (provisional) |
| (c) Trilateration (n fit/fold) | 13.11 | 11.84 | 12.37 | 11.21 | Yes (provisional) |
| (c) Trilateration (fixed n=2.0) | 12.33 | 11.45 | 12.33 | 11.45 | Yes (provisional) |
| (d) KNN fingerprinting | 13.13 | **8.42** | 3.32 | 0.00 | No |

(a) and (d) are training-free apart from the classifier itself, so they run under both
protocols; (b)/(c) don't use point/antenna labels at all, so their LOPO and LOAO rows
differ only in *which rows are scored* (attack-free rows are always excluded, but the
"held-out" concept doesn't otherwise apply — both use the full attack-row RSSI directly).
**Nearest centroid's LOPO median error is exactly 8.42 m** — the chord between adjacent
points — confirming the expected floor: under LOPO the held-out point's centroid does
not exist, so the classifier always lands on a training point, and geometrically the
nearest training point to an unseen one is almost always an immediate ring-neighbour.

WCL and trilateration (10.8-13.1 m, both protocols) sit at essentially the same,
random-guess-adjacent level regardless of protocol — expected, since they don't use
point/antenna labels for anything but scoring; their accuracy is capped by
(1) the accuracy of `SENSOR_POSITIONS`, currently unconfirmed, and (2) how well
non-urban RF propagation follows the log-distance model. Fitting n per fold (mean
n≈2.6, range 1.5-4.0) gives no consistent improvement over the fixed n=2.0 baseline —
another sign that the position-dependent methods are limited primarily by the sensor
geometry, not by n.

## R2.3 — MLP classifier

Added to `eval_protocol.MODEL_CONFIGS` and run through the primary protocol for all 3
tasks × 2 feature sets (appended to `results/table1_primary.csv`, the other 9 models
were not re-run):

| Task | MLP Pooled Acc (Base) | MLP Pooled Acc (FE) | Best of the other 9 (FE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attack Detection | 0.988 | 0.987 | 0.988 (KNN/Random Forest) |
| Point Prediction | 0.688 | 0.718 | 0.743 (KNN) |
| Antenna Prediction | 0.280 | 0.282 | 0.345 (XGBoost) |

MLP is competitive on Attack Detection but consistently below the best tree/boosting
model on Point and Antenna. In the V1a/V1b locked hold-out (`results/final_holdout.csv`),
MLP's dev-only pooled accuracy never exceeded the existing champion for any of the 5
task/design combinations, so it never becomes `selected_on_dev` — the F1 convention
(Task A1) is preserved unchanged: MLP's dev score was computed and compared, but it
lost every comparison, so no other model's flag was touched.

`DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=1)` on `mean_rssi` alone, same primary protocol
(leave-one-block-out). Full table: `results/threshold_detector.csv`.

| Metric | Value |

| Macro-F1 | 0.9851 |
| Precision / Recall | 0.969 / 0.988 |
| FPR | 0.0115 |
| 95% CI (session bootstrap) | [0.984, 0.992] |

| w | False alarms/h | Detection rate | Median delay (s) | Row recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8.31 | 100% (48/48) | 0 | 0.988 |
| 2 | 0.00 | 100% (48/48) | 5 | 0.904 |
| 3 | 0.00 | 100% (48/48) | 10 | 0.820 |

**A single learned RSSI threshold matches the best 9-classifier result (0.988) almost
exactly** — Attack Detection in this dataset is close to linearly separable on
`mean_rssi` alone, which is itself a useful, honest finding for the "insufficient
system-level baseline" critique: the 9-classifier comparison's headline number is not
meaningfully better than the simplest possible detector, and the paper should say so.
